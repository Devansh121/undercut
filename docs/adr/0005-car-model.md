# ADR 0005: Car model built by script in Blender

Status: accepted (M0), updated with what was built (M2). Date: 2026-11-21.

## Context

The car is the biggest risk to the look. It must pass for a current ground-effect single-seater (halo, multi-element front and rear wings, sculpted sidepods, exposed suspension, 18-inch wheels, floor edges), carry ten invented liveries, animate steering, wheel spin, suspension travel, the DRS flap and brake glow, and stay inside the first-race download budget (`docs/asset-survey.md`). Nothing behind a login or paywall may be used, and nothing that copies a real team's car.

## What we looked for

**Freely downloadable CC0 / CC BY models.**

- Poly Haven (522 CC0 models) and ambientCG: no race cars at all; the only car is `covered_car`.
- Kenney Racing Kit (CC0): stylised low-poly toy cars; fails the realism bar, and the download runs through JavaScript.
- OpenGameArt: CC0 karts and low-poly road cars; no modern single-seater.
- Sketchfab: dozens of "downloadable" models of 2022–2026 cars under CC Attribution (CC BY 4.0). The download endpoint refuses anonymous requests (`GET https://api.sketchfab.com/v3/models/<uid>/download` returned 401 on 2026-11-21), so every one of them needs an account. Most are replicas of real team cars with real liveries, or re-uploads of someone else's work, which fails our no-lookalike rule and weakens the licence chain.
- TurboSquid, CGTrader and similar stores: paid or royalty-free licences, not CC; out. AI-model sites: account required and licences unclear; out.

Conclusion: nothing usable downloads without an account. The car has to be made.

## Options for making it

| | Blender by script (headless, Python) | TypeScript lofted surfaces in the engine |
|---|---|---|
| Quality ceiling | High: subdivision surfaces, bevels, booleans, curve extrusions, proper UV unwrapping, baked AO, curvature and normal maps, Cycles reference renders | Medium: lofts and sweeps are fine for wings and the nose; sidepods, the floor and suspension joints need tooling we would have to write (no booleans, no bevel, no bakes) |
| Iteration speed for an agent that only sees renders | Fast: under 1 s to build and export, about 15 s per 1600×1000 Cycles render (256 samples) on the RTX 4050; any angle, any lighting | Fast to rebuild, but each look needs a browser capture, and there is no ground-truth renderer to compare against |
| File size | glTF with Draco (or meshopt) quantisation; LODs built by the same script with fewer sections | Zero download for geometry, but the generator code ships and runs on every device, including phones |
| Liveries | Shared UV layout; per-team masks rendered by the script from `design/teams.json`; decals in an atlas | Same masks possible, but UVs must be generated by our own code |
| Animated parts | Named nodes with pivots on the real axes, exported in the glTF hierarchy; the game drives them | Same, built in code |
| Tooling cost | Blender 5.2 LTS tarball (1.2 GB unpacked) in `~/.cache/undercut/blender/`; build step outside the npm workspace | None beyond the repo |

## Decision

Build the car by script in **Blender 5.2 LTS**, run headless (`blender -b --factory-startup -P tools/car/<part>.py`). Each part is generated from parameters (aerofoil sections lofted along spanwise laws, superellipse body sections, bevelled curve outlines), gets UVs from its own parameters in metres, and is exported to glTF binary with Draco compression. The same scripts produce High and Mobile LODs and the Cycles reference renders that reviewers compare against the game's frames. From M2 the generated `.glb` is committed as a build artefact so the web build does not need Blender; regenerating it is one command. This PR commits only the script and the renders.

## Proof

`tools/car/front_wing.py` (M0; replaced in M2 by the full car in `tools/car/carlib/`) built the front wing assembly: rounded nose tip with a superellipse section that blends into the chassis, four cambered elements (NACA 4-digit sections, inverted) with spanwise chord, sweep, rise and twist laws, flap ends buried in the nose sides, a Gurney strip on the top flap, bevelled curved endplates (carbon inside, painted outside) with a dive vane, carbon twill and clear-coated paint, and the Calder Engineering livery (teal, white side band, red pin line).

Renders (Cycles, OptiX, 256 samples, AgX, studio HDRI from Poly Haven for lighting only): `docs/evidence/m0-car-proof/front_wing_hero.jpg` and `front_wing_top.jpg`. Numbers from `docs/evidence/m0-car-proof/front_wing_stats.json`:

| LOD | Triangles | GLB (Draco) |
|---|---|---|
| hero (renders, cockpit view) | 78,236 | 198 KB |
| game (`--lod game`) | 16,040 | 70 KB |

Build and export take under 1 s; build plus the two renders take about 30 s.

Gaps seen in the proof, to fix in M2: the endplate faces are flat (no bowing, slots or footplate curl); the elements' spanwise waves are too large and the nose crosses them diagonally, so the assembly reads as sculpted rather than regulation-shaped (see the proportion checks in the M2 plan); carbon and the livery band are procedural Blender shaders, which do not survive glTF export, so in the game they become a tiling carbon detail texture and a livery mask (see below); no baked AO yet.

## Consequences

- Every car part is code in `tools/car/`, reviewable and reproducible; no binary art files without a generator.
- Agents iterate by rendering fixed camera stations in Cycles and comparing them with three.js captures of the same `.glb`.
- Blender is a build-time dependency for the car only, fetched as a tarball; CI does not need it because the `.glb` is committed.
- Materials must stay inside what glTF carries (metallic-roughness, clearcoat, normal, occlusion, emissive); procedural looks are baked or moved to the game's shader.

## Plan for the full car in M2

**Parts, in build order:** monocoque and cockpit opening with halo; nose and front wing (this proof, finished); front and rear suspension (aerofoil-section wishbones, push/pull rods, track rods) and uprights with brake ducts; 18-inch wheels with low-profile tyres, sidewall lettering and wheel covers; sidepods with inlets and the undercut; engine cover with airbox, roll hoop and cooling louvres; floor with edge wings, under-floor fences and diffuser; rear wing with beam wing, endplates, DRS flap and actuator pod; rear crash structure and rain light; mirrors; steering wheel with display; driver helmet and shoulders; T-cam and antennae.

**Proportion checks before detail:** the proof wing avoids primitives, but its elements carry large sinusoidal waves and the nose crosses them diagonally, so it reads as a sculpture rather than a current front wing. In M2 every part is shaped against orthographic proportion checks (plan, side and front) written into its script as dimensions taken from the technical regulations' bounding boxes (overall width, element count and chord limits, endplate and nose envelopes, wheelbase, ride heights), and the script asserts them on every build. Each part is then reviewed from three-quarter front, side and top renders, compared with the regulation outlines, before work moves to the next part.

**Triangle budget:**

| | Player car (LOD0) | Near cars (LOD1) | Far cars (LOD2) | Grid total (1 + 5 near + 14 far) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High | 120k | 45k | 12k | about 510k |
| Mobile | 40k | 12k | 4k | about 156k |

**Textures (shared by the grid):** one UV layout for all teams; per-team livery mask at 1024² (R primary, G secondary, B accent, A paint versus bare carbon) generated by the script from `design/teams.json` and the team's `liveryPattern`; one 2048² decal atlas (car numbers, invented sponsor marks, driver codes); shared detail tiles: carbon twill normal 512², tyre sidewall 1024², brake disc emissive ramp 256². All KTX2; about 2 MB for ten teams.

**Livery scheme:** paint colour comes from team tokens at runtime, so a livery is a mask plus three colours, and new teams cost one mask. Patterns stay away from real colour-and-pattern pairings (checked against the real grid when masks are added).

**Animated parts:** the script names nodes and sets pivots on the physical axes: `wheel_FL/FR/RL/RR` (spin about the axle), `steer_FL/FR` (kingpin axis), `upright_*` with suspension travel along the damper axis and wishbones that follow by two-bone constraint in the game, `steering_wheel` (column axis, display as a canvas texture), `drs_flap` (hinge axis, 0–85 mm slot opening), `brake_disc_*` (emissive driven by brake temperature), `rain_light` (emissive).

## M2: what was built

The whole car is now generated by `tools/car/` (`npm run car:build`, about 25 s, no GPU). Hard points are named constants with build-time assertions (`tools/car/carlib/dims.py`, sources in `tools/car/DIMENSIONS.md`). Bodywork is lofted from monotone-spline section laws: one continuous loft for nose, survival cell and engine cover with the cockpit cut along a grid column, separate lofts for the sidepods (raked inlet, undercut, downwash ramp, coke-bottle exit), a tent-section airbox, floor with raised leading edge, fences, edge wings and diffuser, and NACA-section wings on smooth spanwise laws (no waves: the stack only rises and twists towards the tips). Suspension links are streamlined legs between pick-up points; inboard points are projected onto the chassis so no link ends in the air. The review history is `docs/evidence/m2-car/review-log.md`.

**Export.** glTF 2.0 binary, Draco geometry (three.js's stock `DRACOLoader`; the decoder ships with three.js), WebP textures, metres, +Y up, +X forward, origin on the ground under the centre of the wheelbase. Draco rather than meshopt because Blender's exporter writes Draco natively and the build stays a single tool; the cost is the 0.3 MB decoder, loaded once. Textures are WebP rather than KTX2: the loader path for KTX2 (Basis transcoder) is not set up in the client yet and the whole car with all LODs is 1.8 MB, well under the 6 MB allowance.

**Levels of detail and budget.**

| | Triangles | Budget (High preset) | Draw calls | GLB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High | 110,912 | 120k | 82 | 0.88 MB |
| Mobile | 33,790 | 40k (about a quarter of High) | 67 | 0.43 MB |
| Far | 14,268 | 12k: **2.3k over** | 21 | 0.24 MB |

The far LOD is over because the tyre profile, wing sections and body rings have minimum counts below which the silhouette breaks; it is still a tenth of High and 21 draw calls. Textures: carbon twill normal and base 512² (256² in the lower LODs), tyre sidewall 2048 × 512 (1024 × 256), disc glow ramp, livery masks 1024² × 7. Ambient occlusion is baked per vertex (ray cast against the car and the ground) into `COLOR_0`, not into textures, because carbon and suspension parts have no unique UVs.

**Livery scheme: texture masks in a shared atlas, not vertex colours.** Every painted surface is a grid patch packed into one UV atlas (UV0). For each of the seven patterns in `design/teams.json` the build draws a mask by evaluating the pattern at the 3D position and normal of every texel, so stripes and chevrons run continuously across nose, sidepods and engine cover. R = secondary, G = accent, B = bare carbon, primary is the remainder; the renderer mixes the team's three colours in the shader. Masks were chosen over vertex-colour channels because the patterns have hard, thin edges (pin lines 8–16 mm wide) that vertex colours could only hold by cutting the mesh along every pattern, and that cut would differ per pattern; a new pattern now costs one 25–60 KB PNG and no geometry. Ten teams share seven masks. Race numbers and driver codes are not painted: five decal carriers take canvas textures drawn from team data at runtime. Tyre markings use an invented maker's mark; the compound colour is applied to the markings in the shader.

Two patterns were changed after a look at all ten liveries because they came close to real cars: the diagonal split now puts primary forward and secondary low at the rear (not a pale nose and dark rear), and the sidepod block is a panel on the flank rather than the whole sidepod.

**Node contract.** Named nodes with pivots on their physical axes and identity rest rotations: `suspension_*` (travel along +Y), `steer_FL/FR` (kingpin, +Y), `wheel_*` (spin about +Z), `upright_*`, `brake_disc_*` (emissive intensity from temperature), `arm_*` and `driveshaft_*` (pivot inboard, follow the joint given in their extras), `drs_flap` (trailing-edge hinge, +Z, 22°), `steering_wheel` (column axis in extras), `helmet` (yaw), `rain_light`. Full details, signs and LOD differences: `docs/car-asset.md`. A working reference is `tools/car/preview/car-rig.js`.

**Checked in the real renderer.** `tools/car/preview/` is a standalone three.js viewer (environment generated in code, ground with contact shadow, ACES tone mapping, shadowed key light). `npm run car:preview` captures it with Playwright's Chromium on the GPU: all ten liveries, the five standard angles, front-wing and cockpit close-ups, Mobile and far LODs from a chase camera, and posed frames (steer, DRS, bump, brake glow, rain light). Captures are in `docs/evidence/m2-car/threejs/`, Cycles renders in `blender/`, proportion sheets in `ortho/`.

**Remaining giveaways** (full list in the review log): overlapping panels meet in creases rather than fillets; the bodywork has no panel gaps, louvres or small fittings; the rear-wing endplates are plain plates and the wingtips do not roll into them; front-wing elements end square inside the endplates; mirrors are simple; the cockpit has no hands or harness; wheel covers hide a rim without spokes; per-vertex AO is soft on long panels.
