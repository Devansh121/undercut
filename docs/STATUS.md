# Status

```sh
./start.sh
```

## What changed

- M2 interface: a launch screen, then a main menu on one designed kit (tokens, icon set, prompt glyphs that follow the device, components) with the five modes, circuit select (Monza, Spa-Francorchamps and Singapore Marina Bay, each with its plan, length, corners, direction and the record on this device), team and driver pick with a studio image of the car in that livery, and session settings with per-device assist presets. Time Trial runs at any of the three circuits; Online Rooms opens the lobby; Quick Race, Race Weekend and Championship say plainly that they arrive with the race screen. The time-trial HUD is a timing tower top left with the map in the right column. Keyboard, gamepad (D-pad, A, B, LB and RB or Q and E for modes) and touch reach every control. Evidence: `docs/evidence/m2-ui/`.
- M4 racing lines: Spa (medium trim) and Singapore (high trim) have racing lines, speed profiles and reference laps built with `npm run ai:build` like Monza. Spa 109.072 s against the 1:40.562 pole (+8.5 %, a recorded gap, mostly in the downforce sector), Singapore 91.817 s against 1:29.158 (+3.0 %, inside its band), both clean; ten-lap soaks nightly. `npm run telemetry -- lap --circuit spa` prints the tables (docs/physics-validation.md 7.8). The circuits are not in the game's menus yet.
- M5 online client: Online rooms on the launch screen, create or join by invite, ready countdown, server-clock start lights, remote cars and minimap, official results and rematch. A desktop host and landscape phone guest complete the two-browser acceptance test against the local service.
- Controls list: a Controls panel in the pause menu and on the launch screen lists every driving input for the device in use (keyboard, gamepad or touch), read from the live bindings, with the reverse-gear rule. The first start of a page session shows a six-second HUD hint with the brake and throttle inputs and how to open the list.
- M6 Cloudflare deploy: top-level `wrangler.jsonc` composes the built client (staged into `dist/cloudflare` with `tools/deploy/_headers`) and the `/rooms` Durable Objects API on one origin, with `npm run deploy`, `npm run deploy:check` (`wrangler deploy --dry-run` assertions in the `build` CI job), composed-Worker runtime tests (`npm run test:deploy`), SQLite-persisted creation rate limits, a global creation cap, same-origin enforcement and a 4-hour room lifetime cap. Local `./start.sh` and `services/rooms/wrangler.jsonc` are unchanged. See `docs/DEPLOY.md`.
- M3 race core: a pure `packages/race` runs the race rules for every session type (time trial, practice, qualifying, race, race weekend, championship): five-light start with a seeded hold and jump starts, timing from the circuit's control line with mini-sector gaps, track limits with strikes and penalties, yellow, blue, chequered and black-and-white flags, race DRS, ERS laps, pit stops with the limiter, boxes, service time and penalties served, results and points, JSON snapshots, and a stuck-car watchdog. AI cars launch, race (as ghosts to each other: no contact yet), make their pit stops and finish. Rules and values: `docs/race-rules.md`.
- Headless races: `npm run telemetry -- race --circuit monza --cars 20 --laps 5 --seed 1` (and `weekend`) prints the classification, the event log and the watchdog's findings and writes JSON to `artifacts/telemetry/`. Twenty cars run about 22× faster than real time.
- Time trial now runs on the race engine. Laps are timed from Monza's control line (320.5 m before the start line), so the rolling start sits 160 m before it. Records kept before this change were timed from the start line and are dropped once.
- M2 rendering: the game now draws with the real car and real surfaces under a photographed sky. HDR pipeline (MSAA, half-resolution ground-truth AO, bloom on what is actually bright, Khronos PBR Neutral tone map, SMAA, edge speed blur), a CC0 HDRI that lights the scene with its measured sun at Monza's bearing, three cascades of sun shadow to 300 m, height haze. Track surfaces from CC0 texture sets with a rubbered racing line, marbles, worn kerbs and paint, oil and launch marks on the grid. The car asset with liveries, numbers, every moving part driven by the sim and glowing brake discs; other cars instanced (a full grid of twenty holds 60 fps at 1080p High). Ultra, High, Medium and Mobile presets with a first-run benchmark, dynamic resolution and a Graphics setting in the pause menu; a loading screen with progress and Try again. Hooks for the trackside dressing (prop registry, board material, time of day, floodlights). Details in `docs/render.md`, evidence in `docs/evidence/m2-render/`.
- M1 drive: Time Trial at Monza is playable. From the launch screen, Start puts the car on the back of the grid rolling at 150 km/h; the lap clock starts at the line. Keyboard, gamepad and touch all drive it, with assists from the device presets (changeable in the pause menu).
- Session rules: lap and three-sector timing to the millisecond (crossings interpolated inside the 4.2 ms tick), purple/green/yellow sector and lap colours, lap invalidated when all four wheels are past the white line, DRS only inside the two DRS zones, ERS lap counters and fuel reset at the line. The best lap (with its own splits and ghost) and the best time in each sector are saved per circuit (Web Storage; in memory when storage is blocked or full, and the HUD and pause menu say so) and the ghost drives the next laps as a translucent car with a live delta.

The steering wheel's display shows gear, speed and rev lights in the cockpit view; on a phone the chase cameras frame the car above the driver cluster. Lays out at 1440×900 and 844×390 with safe-area insets.
- Engine and road sound from `packages/audio`: the engine is created on the first gesture (the Start click or a first key or touch), follows the car every frame and suspends on pause, tab hide and quit; master, engine and effects volume sliders in the pause menu are saved with the settings.
- `window.__undercut` debug API with `?debug=1` (see `apps/web/src/debug.ts`), a keyboard-only autopilot used to prove a full lap, and `npm run shots -- monza` (screenshot stations on the real GPU, `tools/screenshots/`).
- Off-track fix: barriers stop cars. A car that leaves the track now hits the barrier, scrubs speed and slides along it, and the impact speed is reported for damage and audio later. The pit lane stays drivable with the pit wall between it and the track. The ground inside the barriers no longer steps (a lap-projection bug left a 2.7 m step at the first chicane), and the vehicle model survives bad ground anyway: tyre and floor forces saturate, the suspension has stops, and a step in the ground lifts the car instead of launching it. A reusable pose checker (`checkCarInvariants`) backs fuzz suites on an adversarial surface and on Monza, and the game resets the car if it ever fails (ADR 0009, physics-validation section 7.7).

- The launch screen shows the name, a Drive button (not live until M1) and the build hash over a placeholder 3D view.
- M5 rooms service and protocol: private rooms by invite code on the local Workers runtime, with the server owning the clock, grid, start lights, phases, timing checks and results; protocol v2 with binary car snapshots; a client library with clock sync, reconnect and remote-car interpolation; host migration with AI handover. The lobby and race UI now use the same client library as the headless soak. See `docs/netcode.md`.

## Try this

M2 rendering, three things to look at:

1. Graphics in the pause menu: switch between Ultra, High, Medium and Mobile and watch the loading screen, then the shadows' reach and the ambient occlusion under the car change. A first run on a new browser picks a preset by itself after five seconds of driving.
2. Brake hard into the first chicane from 300 km/h in the near chase camera and look at the front brake discs (they glow), the rubbered line and marbles off line, and the kerbs as you clip them.
3. Add `?tod=night-floodlit` or `?tod=overcast-day` to the address: the same circuit at night under floodlights and on an overcast day.

Keys: arrows or WASD drive, Q/E shift (gears are automatic by default), F asks for DRS, Space overtake, C camera, B look back, Esc pause, Backspace reset to track.

1. Trail-brake into the first chicane (Variante del Rettifilo): brake hard and straight from about the 150 m board, then ease off the brake as you turn in. With ABS on you can stand on it; switch ABS off in the pause menu and feel the fronts lock if you turn while braking hard.
2. Traction out of the second chicane (Variante della Roggia) with traction control off: set TC to Off in the pause menu, use manual gears, and feed the throttle in second gear. Too much too early and the rear steps out.
3. DRS on the main straight: after Parabolica press F once the DRS box on the HUD turns white (zone open), watch it go green and the rear flap open, and note the extra top speed; the first brake press closes it.
4. On a phone in landscape, open the `Wi-Fi:` HTTPS address: steer with the left pad, pedals on the right. Everything should fit around the controls.

## Decided for you

- Folded run-off at the first chicane (M4): a general fill of the folded run-off (a plane through the surrounding track edges plus the smoothest correction) was built and measured but not merged: it makes 28 open kerb-review cases and the turn 2 run pass and halves Monza's worst run-off slope, but three cases that pass on master reset (game teleport(5600, 0) at TC 0 and 2, sim seed 97513), Singapore's reference lap is 3 ms slower, and two of the three cases that block the low-trim downforce change still reset at ClA 3.9. With the chicane's 3 % grade the pockets between its turns must climb 10–15 %, so a correct ground and those zero-reset assertions conflict; whether to trade the three for the 28 is the owner's call. The code is on branch `m4/chicane-ground-fill`; the measurements are in `docs/track-data.md` 5.7. The downforce retry was not attempted.

- Physics gaps (M4, PR 38): the car is unchanged. Matching the published brake powers (a 22.5–23 mm master cylinder) and raising the low and medium downforce to the cited team figure ("three or four times the weight" at top speed) brought the laps to about 82.5 / 107.1–107.4 / 90.5–90.7 s, but each moved a check that passes on master (the 330 km/h lock, the Monza T1 2025 stop time, the Spa T5 2025 peak and trap, three PR 19 kerb cases, the clumsy keyboard and autopilot laps). Per the owner's rule the passing checks win: the four brake powers are validation targets recorded as gaps, and the trial is in `docs/physics-validation.md` section 7.9. OpenF1 telemetry is CC BY-NC-SA, so it was looked at only privately and nothing in the repository rests on it.

- Menus without a framework (ADR 0009). The team cars on the main menu are pre-rendered studio images of the real car model, not a live 3D view. Label text was raised to `#8d8c88` so it meets 4.5:1 on every panel. Time trial's tower shows the green flag only: time trial has no other flags. The controls list opens from the menu's top bar and the pause menu.
- Drivability (PR 26): the stability assist is yaw-rate control that brakes one wheel and trims engine torque through a new optional per-wheel brake demand in `ControlInput`; it never steers or eases the brake. On grass or gravel it keeps braking the outer front even when that wheel locks (a locked rear is still refused). Braking help, already a preset field, is implemented from the driving line and is on by default: `low` for keyboard, `medium` for touch, off for gamepad. On the review's clumsy keyboard driver it brakes about 13 s a lap and accounts for most of the drop in barrier strikes. Online rooms can switch both off. The validation harness charges a spin 5 s and counts it as time off the track, and resets a car stopped for 3 s as a spin; without that charge, saving a slide reads as more time off the track than a spin.
- Online Slice 1 uses deterministic seat liveries, equal cars and no AI fill, without extending protocol v2. Everyone marks ready (including the host) to begin the server countdown; the host can cancel by unreadying.
- Invite links use the local server’s Wi-Fi origin when available; the same-origin network-info endpoint supplies only interface addresses. Outside that server the page origin remains usable.
- Start release happens at the first fixed simulation tick on or after server lights-out. Each frame maps its catch-up ticks to the elapsed server-clock interval, so ticks before lights-out remain held even when rendering stalls. The acceptance test compares the actual released ticks within one 240 Hz tick plus clock error, using the normal referee floors.
- M6 audio Slice 1 uses synthesized car/road layers and estimated camera/gear-mesh balances.

  until spatial voices land in Slice 2. Collision audio accepts explicit speed/serial events;
  current master has no barrier or car-contact telemetry, so the seam stays silent by default.

It is driven by an ideal, telemetry-driven reference controller: the car's built-in TC and ABS are off, but the controller reads exact slips, loads and sideslip every tick, regulates the pedals from slip and recovers from slides instantly. It is a measuring instrument, not a model of a human. - Physics calibration: the tyre's longitudinal and lateral friction are set separately; both load slopes are −8.75e-5 per N from Perantoni and Limebeer's published single-seater tyre table, not fitted. The high-downforce braking peaks above the published ones are recorded as gaps with competing explanations, not absorbed by the tyre. Targets carry one of three roles (direct fit, selection check, withheld validation). Low-trim ClA stays at the table's 3.6 for want of evidence to raise it. Counting engine braking inside the brake-by-wire rear demand was tried and not adopted (it needs a brake-migration model with a source).
- Contact model: each tyre deflects against the ground averaged over its whole contact patch (a 5 × 2 grid over 0.32/0.40 m by 0.15 m), sampled once per 240 Hz step at the wheel's end-of-step position and interpolated over the substeps; its carcass damping grows in over the first 3 mm of deflection, so kerb strikes are about half as hard and an exit kerb no longer lifts a wheel. A three-probe shortcut was removed: it missed longitudinal and oblique steps. Cost on Monza: 0.38 ms per 20-car tick (0.17 ms with point contact, 0.75 ms sampling every substep), measured by `npm run telemetry -- bench`. The body damping (7 kN·s/m per wheel) is lumped, phenomenological damping at the tyre, not a physical tyre property; the open item is to revisit the wheel rates and add a heave element so it can move to the suspension (physics-validation 1.6).
- Track limits have one definition in `packages/ai/src/line/limits.ts`, the game's own rule (a wheel is off when its tyre is wholly past the white line; a lap is invalid when all four are). The reference line's corridor is built from the car's footprint: at every heading within ±10° and with 0.3 m of outward tracking error, one wheel must still touch the line and every corner of every tyre's patch must lie on track, a kerb inside its crest or asphalt run-off, 0.2 m clear of grass and gravel. The lap runner counts wheels off by the same rule and samples every tyre's centre and tread edges for grass and gravel; the reference lap and the soak assert none (the earlier centre-only corridor put tyres on grass on every lap).
- The reference driver countersteers and comes off the brake in a slide, shapes its lock threshold to each tyre's combined slip, and plans at 98 % of the lateral and traction limits and 95 % of braking (higher is slower). Brake bias stays fixed at 57 %: no source was found for how the real cars' brake migration is set.
- The timed lap follows one out-lap on which the MGU-K is saved (a full store at the line, as in qualifying). Shorter run-ups placed the car at full speed with a full store just before a braking zone, a state a flying lap never reaches, and it could spin there. Track limits are judged strictly: a wheel is off once its centre is past the white line.
- Monza laps and sectors are timed from the control line, measured on the FIA circuit map at s = 5472.5 m (320.5 m before the track file's s = 0, ±13 m). The track file is unchanged; the track pipeline should carry the control line as metadata, and its DRS zone 2 activation belongs at s = 5484.5 m (12 m after the control line on the map), not the stored 12 m, 320.5 m late. Measured with a labelled harness override (`npm run telemetry -- calibrate --drs2 5484.5`), it is worth 0.097 s; the committed lap and tests use the track file as it is.
- The nightly ten-lap soak starts with 34 kg of fuel (qualifying fuel cannot cover ten laps) and requires a lap-time spread of at most 0.5 s; measured 0.354 s with the lap-band controller (0.228 s on the first car).
- Low-trim ClA at the top of its range (4.2 m², drag and tyre refitted) was investigated and not adopted: it took the lap to 84.036 s and closed two targets but opened another, and at 300 km/h it puts the floor's leading edge at 19.3 mm, below the 20 mm stall height the aero map is built on. The table's 3.6 stays (physics-validation 7.3).
- Open item: re-measuring the ride heights the aero map is referenced to moves the Spa T5 2025 peak to 5.101 g, 0.001 g past its band; left as they are (physics-validation 7.3).
- The racing-line and envelope artefacts are checked for staleness by behavioural fingerprints (what the sim, track and line code compute), not by hashing source text, so unrelated edits do not block other work. The check takes about 5 s in the physics job.

- Time trial starts rolling (150 km/h) from the last grid slot, so lap 1 is a near-flying lap.
- Timing colours in time trial: purple beats the record kept on this device, green is this session's best, yellow is slower, red is an invalid lap.
- Monza runs the sim's low-drag aero trim and a 20 kg fuel load, topped up at every line crossing so laps compare fairly.
- A lap is invalid when all four tyres are wholly past the white line (half tread 0.18 m); reset to track also invalidates the lap. No penalties until M3.
