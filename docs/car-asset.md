# The car asset: loading, livery, animation

For the renderer. The files are generated by `npm run car:build` (see `tools/car/README.md`); do not edit them by hand. A working reference of everything on this page is `tools/car/preview/car-rig.js` (used by the preview viewer); port it rather than re-deriving it.

All under `apps/web/public/assets/car/` (served at `assets/car/` from the client build):

## Loading

glTF 2.0 binary with Draco geometry compression (`KHR_draco_mesh_compression`), WebP textures (`EXT_texture_webp`) and `KHR_texture_transform`; all three are handled by three.js's stock `GLTFLoader`. Draco needs the decoder that ships with three.js (`three/examples/jsm/libs/draco/gltf/`): copy or serve that folder. Use one `DRACOLoader` for all the car files and call `dispose()` on it when the loads finish or fail; it holds a worker pool. `loadCars()` in `car-rig.js` does this.

```js
const draco = new DRACOLoader().setDecoderPath(dracoUrl);
const loader = new GLTFLoader().setDRACOLoader(draco);
try {
  const [high, mobile, far] = await Promise.all(
    ['high', 'mobile', 'far'].map((l) => loader.loadAsync(`assets/car/car_${l}.glb`)),
  );
} finally {
  draco.dispose();
}
```

Frame and scale: metres, +Y up, +X forward, +Z to the car's right (the body frame in `AGENTS.md`). The `car` node's origin is on the ground under the centre of the wheelbase, with identity rotation and scale. Place it with the sim's chassis pose directly: no extra rotation or offset. Every other node also has identity rotation at rest; its translation is its pivot.

Vertex data: painted parts have `TEXCOORD_0` (the livery atlas). Most parts also have `TEXCOORD_1` (metric UVs for the carbon weave and the tyre and disc textures) and `COLOR_0` (ambient occlusion baked per vertex). `GLTFLoader` turns on `vertexColors` where `COLOR_0` exists, which multiplies it into the base colour; keep that. The decal carriers have only `TEXCOORD_0` and no vertex colours.

## Livery

The painted parts (material `paint`: bodywork, wings, DRS flap, halo, wheel covers, helmet, the driver's suit and gloves) are recoloured at runtime. One mesh serves all ten teams.

- Load the team's mask from `livery/index.json` → `teams[id].mask`. It is a data texture: `colorSpace = NoColorSpace`, `flipY = false` (glTF UV convention), sampled with `TEXCOORD_0`.
- Channels: **R** = secondary colour weight, **G** = accent colour weight, **B** = bare carbon (the paint is left off). Primary is `1 − R − G`.
- Shader: `paint = primary·(1−R−G) + secondary·R + accent·G`, then `colour = mix(paint, carbon #141518, B)`, with roughness mixed towards 0.5 by B. Colours come from `index.json` (sRGB hex; three.js converts them to linear). `liveryMaterial()` in `car-rig.js` does this with `onBeforeCompile`: it sets the mask as `material.map` and replaces `map_fragment`.
- Clone the `paint` material per car, not per mesh.
- Each team has its own mask: ten designs, not one pattern recoloured. The masks are mirrored (left and right share texels), and the four wheel covers share one patch.
- The `paint` material also carries a normal map on `TEXCOORD_0`: panel gaps, fasteners and cooling louvres. Leave it on.

Without a mask the file shows a neutral grey primer, on purpose: no team is baked into the mesh.

## Markings: race numbers and driver codes

One node, `decals`: five carriers in one mesh (nose top, both engine-cover flanks, both nose flanks), shrink-wrapped 1.5 mm off the bodywork. Its `extras.decal` gives the canvas size (`[2048, 512]`) and the regions: carriers for the race number read `u` 0–0.4 of the canvas, those for the driver code `u` 0.4–1 (`v` 0–1 up the text). In the file the material is fully transparent (`alphaMode MASK`, alpha 0).

Draw the number centred in the left region and the code centred in the right one on a transparent canvas. Make a `CanvasTexture` with `flipY = false` and `colorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`, and give the node a transparent material with `polygonOffset` (factor and units −2) and `depthWrite = false`. Use the UI's number family (Barlow Semi Condensed Bold, `design/mock/fonts/`). The preview picks light or dark text from the primary colour's luminance. The far LOD has no decals.

## Tyre compounds

The compound system is data in `design/compounds.json`: name, code letter, colour and mark count per compound (soft magenta with three marks, medium orange with two, hard steel blue with one). On the tyre, each sidewall carries the invented maker's mark once and four groups of three short radial ticks. The renderer lights the ticks in the slots the compound lists in its colour and leaves the others as rubber; the lettering stays neutral grey. `setCompound()` in `car-rig.js` decodes the slot from `TEXCOORD_1.u` (`groupsPerSidewall` and `slotSpacingU` are in the JSON). Do not tint the whole tyre material; the rubber would go brown.

## Animated nodes

Names are the same in all LODs except where noted. Signs follow the right-hand rule about the given axis, in the parent's frame.

| Node | Pivot | Motion | Rest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `suspension_FL/FR/RL/RR` | Wheel centre | Translate along +Y: suspension travel, positive = bump (wheel up relative to the body) | Static ride height, wheel centre 0.360 m above the ground |
| `steer_FL/FR` | Kingpin, vertical, 0.138 m inboard of the wheel centre | Rotate about +Y: road-wheel steer angle, positive = left | Straight ahead |
| `wheel_FL/FR/RL/RR` | Wheel centre (axle) | Rotate about +Z. Rolling forward is **negative**: `rotation.z -= v / 0.36 · dt`. Carries tyre, rim and wheel cover | Arbitrary |
| `upright_*` | Wheel centre, under `steer_*` (front) or `suspension_*` (rear) | Does not spin. Carries the brake duct, caliper, wheel-wake deflector and the brake disc (a smooth solid of revolution, so it needs no spin). Brake glow: clone the `brake_disc` material per corner and set its `emissiveIntensity` (0 = cold, about 4 = glowing). The file's emissive factor is 1 with a ramp texture, so the renderer **must** set the intensity, starting at 0. Not in the far LOD | |
| `suspension_links` | Car origin | All wishbones, track rods, push and pull rods, toe links and driveshafts in one mesh with six morph targets: `bump_FL/FR/RL/RR` (that corner at `extras.morph.bump_ref_m` = 0.03 m of bump) and `steer_FL/FR` (that front corner at `extras.morph.steer_ref_rad` = 0.35 rad of left steer). Set `influence = travel / 0.03` and `steer / 0.35`; negative influences give droop and right steer. Each target is the links rotated rigidly about their inboard joints, so the blend stays within about 1 mm over ±30 mm and ±20°. Not in the far LOD | All zero |
| `drs_flap` | Spanwise hinge at the flap trailing edge | Rotate about +Z by `extras.open_deg` (22°) to open: the leading edge rises and the slot opens about 85 mm. The flap's tips stay in the static rolled wingtips | Closed |
| `steering_wheel` | Wheel centre | Rotate about `extras.axis` (the column, pointing at the driver), positive = left. Carries the driver's gloves. A material `display` on its face is the dash display: set an emissive `CanvasTexture` (UV1 0..1 across the screen). Not in the far LOD | Centred |
| `helmet` | Neck, 0.10 m below the helmet centre | Rotate about +Y: head yaw, positive = left | Facing forward |
| `rain_light` | Lamp behind the bezel on the crash-structure tip | Material `rain_light`: set `emissive` to red and drive `emissiveIntensity` (0 off) | Off |

## Levels of detail

Switch by the car's projected size. With a bounding sphere of radius R = 3.0 m about the car origin, a camera at distance d with vertical field of view `fov`, and a viewport `H` pixels tall, the projected radius is

`r_px = R · H / (2 · d · tan(fov / 2))`

| Level | Use when | High preset | Mobile preset |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High | r_px ≥ 160 | Player car and close cars | Not used |
| Mobile | 50 ≤ r_px < 160 | Mid-distance cars | Player car and close cars (r_px ≥ 50) |
| Far | r_px < 50 | Distant cars | Distant cars |

Switch down at the threshold and back up only at 1.15 × the threshold, so a car at the boundary does not flicker. `docs/evidence/m2-car/threejs/lod_strip_high_mobile.jpg` and `lod_strip_mobile_far.jpg` show the three levels side by side at both switch sizes (top row actual pixels, bottom row magnified). The levels share the livery atlas, the masks and the tyre texture layout, so paint boundaries and tyre markings stay put across a switch. The tyre outline is 64 segments on High, 40 on Mobile and 16 on Far.

| | High | Mobile | Far |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Moving nodes | All | All | Wheels, steer, suspension, DRS, helmet, rain light; no uprights, links, steering wheel or decals |
| Textures | Carbon 512², tyre 2048 × 512, bodywork detail 1024² | Carbon 256², tyre 1024 × 256, detail 512² | As Mobile |

## Materials

`paint` (clear coated, recoloured as above), `carbon` (twill normal map on UV1, soft clear coat), `carbon_matte` (floor, fences, diffuser), `tyre`, `rim`, `brake_disc`, `metal_dark`, `exhaust`, `dark`, `visor`, `mirror_glass`, `display`, `rain_light`, `decal`. The levels of detail merge minor materials to save draw calls (on Mobile, for example, `carbon_matte` becomes `carbon` and the wheel cover becomes `rim`), so look materials up by name and expect some to be absent.
