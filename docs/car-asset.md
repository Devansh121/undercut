# The car asset: loading, livery, animation

For the renderer. The files are generated by `npm run car:build` (see `tools/car/README.md`); do not edit them by hand. A working reference of everything on this page is `tools/car/preview/car-rig.js` (used by the preview viewer); port it rather than re-deriving it.

## Loading

glTF 2.0 binary with Draco geometry compression (`KHR_draco_mesh_compression`), WebP textures (`EXT_texture_webp`) and `KHR_texture_transform`; all three are handled by three.js's stock `GLTFLoader`. Draco needs the decoder that ships with three.js (`three/examples/jsm/libs/draco/gltf/`): copy or serve that folder and pass it to `DRACOLoader.setDecoderPath`.

```js
const draco = new DRACOLoader().setDecoderPath(dracoUrl);
const gltf = await new GLTFLoader().setDRACOLoader(draco).loadAsync('assets/car/car_high.glb');
const car = gltf.scene.getObjectByName('car');
```

Frame and scale: metres, +Y up, +X forward, +Z to the car's right (the body frame in `AGENTS.md`). The `car` node's origin is on the ground under the centre of the wheelbase, with identity rotation and scale. Place it with the sim's chassis pose directly: no extra rotation or offset. Every other node also has identity rotation at rest; its translation is its pivot.

Each mesh primitive has `NORMAL`, `TEXCOORD_0` (livery atlas, used by painted parts), `TEXCOORD_1` (metric UVs for the carbon weave and the tyre and disc textures) and `COLOR_0` (baked ambient occlusion; `GLTFLoader` turns on `vertexColors`, which multiplies it into the base colour; keep that).

## Livery

The painted bodywork (material `paint`, on the body, the DRS flap, the halo, the wheel covers and the helmet) is recoloured at runtime; one mesh serves all ten teams.

- Load the team's mask from `livery/index.json` → `teams[id].mask`. It is a data texture: `colorSpace = NoColorSpace`, `flipY = false` (glTF UV convention), sampled with `TEXCOORD_0`.
- Channels: **R** = secondary colour weight, **G** = accent colour weight, **B** = bare carbon (the paint is left off). Primary is `1 − R − G`.
- Shader: `paint = primary·(1−R−G) + secondary·R + accent·G`, then `colour = mix(paint, carbon #141518, B)` and roughness mixed towards 0.42 by B. Colours come from `index.json` (sRGB hex; three.js converts them to linear). `liveryMaterial()` in `car-rig.js` does this with `onBeforeCompile` by setting the mask as `material.map` and replacing `map_fragment`.
- Clone the `paint` material per car, not per mesh.
- Masks are mirrored: left and right share texels, and the four wheel covers share one patch.

Without the mask the file shows a neutral grey primer, on purpose: no team is baked into the mesh.

## Markings: race numbers and driver codes

Five decal carriers, shrink-wrapped 1.5 mm off the bodywork: `decal_number_nose` (read from the front), `decal_number_side_L/R` (engine cover flanks), `decal_code_L/R` (nose flanks). Each has `extras.decal` = `"number"` or `"code"` and UVs running 0..1 across it (u to the reader's right, v up the decal). In the file their material is fully transparent (`alphaMode MASK`, alpha 0).

Draw the text into a canvas (number aspect about 1.6:1, code about 3.4:1), make a `CanvasTexture` with `flipY = false` and `colorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`, and give each carrier a transparent material with `polygonOffset` (factor and units −2) and `depthWrite = false`. Use the UI's number family (Barlow Semi Condensed Bold, `design/mock/fonts/`). The preview picks light or dark text from the primary colour's luminance. The far LOD has no decal carriers.

## Animated nodes

Names are identical in all LODs except where noted. Signs follow the right-hand rule about the given axis, in the parent's frame.

| Node | Pivot | Motion | Rest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `suspension_FL/FR/RL/RR` | Wheel centre | Translate along +Y: suspension travel, positive = bump (wheel up relative to the body) | Static ride height, wheel centre 0.360 m above the ground |
| `steer_FL/FR` | Kingpin, vertical, 0.138 m inboard of the wheel centre | Rotate about +Y: road-wheel steer angle, positive = left | Straight ahead |
| `wheel_FL/FR/RL/RR` | Wheel centre (axle) | Rotate about +Z. Rolling forward is **negative**: `rotation.z -= v / 0.36 · dt` | Arbitrary |
| `upright_*` | Wheel centre | Does not spin; carries the brake duct, caliper and wheel-wake deflector. Not in the far LOD | |
| `brake_disc_*` | Wheel centre, child of the wheel (spins with it) | Glow: set the material's `emissiveIntensity` (0 = cold, about 4 = glowing). The file's emissive factor is 1 with a ramp texture, so the renderer **must** set the intensity, starting at 0. Clone the material per corner. Not in the far LOD | |
| `arm_<corner>_lower/upper/trackrod/pushrod` (front), `arm_<corner>_lower/upper/toelink/pullrod` (rear), `driveshaft_RL/RR` | Inboard end of the link (middle of the two inboard joints for a wishbone) | `extras.outboard` is the outboard joint in the car frame at rest; `extras.follows` names the node it is attached to. Each frame, transform that point into the follower's current pose and rotate the link so its rest direction (outboard − pivot) points at it: `quaternion.setFromUnitVectors(rest, current)`. Rigid links; over ±30 mm of travel the joint drifts about 1 mm off the link end, which does not show. Merged into `body` in the far LOD | |
| `drs_flap` | Spanwise hinge at the flap trailing edge | Rotate about +Z by `extras.open_deg` (22°) to open: the leading edge rises, the slot opens about 85 mm | Closed |
| `steering_wheel` | Wheel centre | Rotate about `extras.axis` (the column, pointing at the driver), positive = left. A material `display` on its face is the dash display: set an emissive `CanvasTexture` (UV1 0..1 across the screen). Not in the far LOD | Centred |
| `helmet` | Neck, 0.10 m below the helmet centre | Rotate about +Y: head yaw, positive = left. Small pitch or roll about the same pivot also works | Facing forward |
| `rain_light` | Rear crash structure tip | Material `rain_light`: set `emissive` to red and drive `emissiveIntensity` (0 off) | Off |

Tyre compound: the sidewall markings are the bright texels of the `tyre` base-colour texture. Recolour only those (see `setCompound()` in `car-rig.js`): soft red, medium yellow, hard white. Do not tint the whole material; the rubber would go brown.

## Materials

`paint` (clear-coated, recoloured as above), `carbon` (twill normal map on UV1, clear coat), `carbon_matte` (floor, fences, strakes, edge wings), `tyre`, `rim`, `nut`, `brake_disc`, `caliper`, `metal_dark`, `metal_satin`, `exhaust`, `soot`, `dark`, `interior`, `headrest`, `plank`, `boot`, `grip`, `tcam`, `visor`, `mirror_glass`, `display`, `rain_light`, `decal`. The Mobile and far files merge the minor ones (for example `soot`, `plank`, `headrest` → `dark`) to save draw calls, so look materials up by name defensively.

## Levels of detail

| | High | Mobile | Far |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Use | Player car (High), cockpit view | Player car (Mobile), near cars (High) | Cars in the distance |
| Moving nodes | All | All | Wheels, steer, suspension, DRS, helmet, rain light; links merged into the body; no uprights, discs, steering wheel or decals |
| Textures | Carbon 512², tyre 2048 × 512 | Carbon 256², tyre 1024 × 256 | As Mobile |

The ADR budget is 120k / 40k / 12k triangles for High; High and Mobile are inside it, the far LOD is 14.3k, 2.3k over (see the ADR).
